## Definition and History {#definition-and-history}

### Definition of E-Commerce

E-commerce (electronic commerce) is the **buying and selling of goods and services, and the transfer of money and data, over electronic networks, mainly the Internet**.

It uses websites, mobile apps and other electronic platforms. It includes the paperless exchange of business information through electronic data interchange (EDI), e-mail, electronic funds transfer (EFT) and other network technologies.

The main activities of e-commerce are:

1. Selling products and services online.
2. Buying products and services online.
3. Paying and accepting payments online.
4. Carrying out other business transactions and services online.

**Examples:** online shopping sites and marketplaces (Amazon, eBay, Alibaba, Daraz), online ticket and hotel booking, online banking and bill payment, food delivery apps and the sale of digital products such as e-books, software and online courses.

### Features of E-Commerce

1. **Non-cash payment** — payment by credit and debit cards, smart cards, mobile wallets and bank transfers.
2. **24 × 7 availability** — the business runs and serves customers at any time, from anywhere.
3. **Global reach (no geographical boundary)** — customers all over the world can be reached.
4. **Advertising and marketing** — products and services reach more people at lower cost.
5. **Improved sales** — orders are taken at any time without human intervention.
6. **Support** — pre-sales and post-sales help for customers.
7. **Inventory management** — stock is controlled automatically, and reports are produced when needed.
8. **Communication** — faster, cheaper and more reliable communication with customers and partners.
9. **Information density** — product information and prices are easy to find and compare.
10. **Delivery takes time** — physical goods must still be shipped to the customer.

### How an Online Transaction Works

1. The customer browses the online store, adds products to the shopping cart and places the order.
2. The customer pays online; the payment details go to a **payment gateway**.
3. The payment gateway sends the payment for **authorization** to the customer's bank (through the card network).
4. The bank checks the account and **approves** (or declines) the payment.
5. The payment gateway informs the online store, which **confirms** the order to the customer.
6. The order is processed and packed in the warehouse.
7. The product is **shipped** through a courier.
8. The product is **delivered** to the customer.

E-commerce is classified into types (business models) such as **B2B, B2C, C2C, C2B, B2G and G2C**, according to the parties that take part; these are explained in [E-Commerce Business Models](/topics/software-engineering/e-commerce-business-models).

### History of E-Commerce

1. **1960s–1970s — EDI and EFT:** companies began to exchange business documents electronically with **Electronic Data Interchange (EDI)**, and banks began to transfer money electronically with **Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT)**. These were the first forms of electronic commerce.
2. **1979 — Online shopping invented:** **Michael Aldrich**, an English inventor, demonstrated the first online shopping system by connecting a modified television to a transaction-processing computer through a telephone line.
3. **1982 — Minitel:** France Télécom launched **Minitel** across France, which people used for online ordering.
4. **1984 — CompuServe Electronic Mall:** CompuServe opened the **Electronic Mall** in the USA and Canada, one of the first comprehensive e-commerce services.
5. **1991 — World Wide Web:** the Web (invented by **Tim Berners-Lee**) became publicly available, making online shops possible for everyone.
6. **1994–1995 — Secure transactions:** On **11 August 1994**, **NetMarket** made the first secure (encrypted) online retail sale — a CD of Sting's *Ten Summoner's Tales* — paid by credit card. **Netscape** developed **SSL** in 1994 and released its first public version (SSL 2.0) with its browser in 1995, making online payment secure.
7. **1995 — Amazon and eBay:** **Jeff Bezos** launched **Amazon.com** (first as an online bookstore), and **Pierre Omidyar** founded **eBay** (as AuctionWeb), the first popular online auction site for person-to-person sales. In the same year, the US National Science Foundation lifted its ban on commercial use of the Internet.
8. **1998–1999 — Online payment and marketplaces:** **PayPal** started online payment between individuals, and **Alibaba** was founded in China (1999).
9. **2000s onwards — Growth of m-commerce:** with smartphones, mobile apps and mobile payments, e-commerce moved to mobile devices (m-commerce), and social media became a major channel for selling and advertising.

## E-Commerce vs E-Business {#e-commerce-vs-e-business}

### Definition of E-Business

E-business (electronic business) is the **conducting of all business activities electronically** over the Internet, intranets and extranets.

Besides buying and selling, it includes the **internal processes** of a business, such as procurement (buying raw materials), supply chain management, inventory, production, customer relationship management (CRM), human resource management, accounting and customer service, using systems such as ERP and CRM.

So **e-commerce is a part (subset) of e-business**: e-commerce covers only the commercial transactions (buying, selling and payment), while e-business covers the whole running of the business.

### Difference Between E-Commerce and E-Business

| Basis | E-Commerce | E-Business |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning | Buying and selling of goods and services, and making payments, over the Internet | Conducting all business activities electronically |
| Scope | Narrower concept; a **subset** of e-business | Broader concept; a **superset** of e-commerce |
| Activities | Commercial transactions only: buying, selling, online payment | All business activities: procurement, supply chain, production, inventory, CRM, HR, accounting, customer service, as well as buying and selling |
| Transactions | Limited to monetary (commercial) transactions | Not limited; includes non-monetary activities |
| Business processes | External processes only (dealing with customers and suppliers) | Both internal and external processes |
| Network used | Internet | Internet, intranet and extranet |
| Systems needed | Usually a website or app | Websites plus integrated systems such as ERP, CRM and SCM |
| Best suited for | Business-to-consumer (B2C) | Business-to-business (B2B) |
| Example | A customer buying a book from an online store | A company handling its suppliers, stock, staff, accounts and sales through connected systems |

## Advantages and Disadvantages {#advantages-and-disadvantages}

### Advantages of E-Commerce

#### Advantages to Organizations

1. **Wider market** — the business can reach national and international markets with little capital investment.
2. **More customers, suppliers and partners** can be found all over the world.
3. **Lower costs** — no physical shop is needed; the cost of rent, staff and paper-based information is reduced.
4. **Open 24 × 7** — sales continue at all hours.
5. **Better customer service** and a stronger brand image.
6. **Faster, simpler business processes** with less paperwork.
7. **Better inventory control and higher productivity** — supports "pull"-type supply management and just-in-time production.
8. **Low-cost advertising and market research** through the Internet.

#### Advantages to Consumers

1. **Shopping at any time, from anywhere** (24 × 7), without visiting a shop.
2. **More choices** of products and sellers.
3. **Easy comparison** of products and prices to find cheaper or better options.
4. **Lower prices and discounts**, because of competition and fewer intermediaries.
5. **Reviews and detailed product information** are available within seconds.
6. **Home delivery** and fast delivery of digital products.
7. **Virtual auctions** can be joined.

#### Advantages to Society

1. **Less travel** to shops reduces traffic and air pollution.
2. **Lower prices** help less well-off people to buy products.
3. **People in rural and remote areas** get products and services that are not available locally.
4. **Public services** such as health care, education and government services can be delivered online at lower cost.

### Disadvantages of E-Commerce

#### Technical Disadvantages

1. **Security and reliability problems** — poorly implemented systems can be hacked or can fail.
2. **Low bandwidth** of Internet connections in some areas.
3. **Special software and web servers** may be needed, besides the network servers.
4. **Integration difficulty** — it can be hard to connect e-commerce software with existing applications and databases.
5. **Compatibility problems** — some software or hardware may not work with some operating systems or devices.
6. **Rapid change** in technology and software makes systems outdated quickly.

#### Non-Technical Disadvantages

1. **High initial cost** of building and maintaining an e-commerce system, and delays due to lack of experience.
2. **Security and privacy risks** — fraud, hacking, card-data theft, phishing and misuse of personal information.
3. **Lack of trust (user resistance)** — customers may not trust an unknown, faceless seller and may prefer traditional shops.
4. **No physical inspection** — customers cannot touch, feel or try a product before buying.
5. **Delivery delays, damaged goods and wrong descriptions** lead to dissatisfaction and returns.
6. **Dependence on the Internet** — not everyone has Internet access, and access is costly or poor in remote areas.
7. **Less personal relationship** between buyer and seller.
8. **Legal problems** — online fraud, disputes between parties who have never met, and different laws in different countries.
