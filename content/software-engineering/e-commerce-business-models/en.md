## B2B, B2C, C2C, C2B {#b2b-b2c-c2c-c2b}

### E-Commerce Business Model

An e-commerce business model is the **way buying and selling take place online**, classified by **who sells** and **who buys**.

Each model is named with two letters joined by "2" ("to"): the first letter is the seller and the last letter is the buyer — **B** = Business, **C** = Consumer, **G** = Government.

The main e-commerce business models are seven:

1. **B2B** — Business-to-Business
2. **B2C** — Business-to-Consumer
3. **C2C** — Consumer-to-Consumer
4. **C2B** — Consumer-to-Business
5. **B2G** — Business-to-Government
6. **G2B** — Government-to-Business
7. **G2C** — Government-to-Citizen (Consumer)

### B2B (Business-to-Business)

B2B is the e-commerce model in which **both the buyer and the seller are businesses**.

One business sells products, raw materials or services to another business, which uses them in production or **resells** them to the final customer.

**How it works:** a business buyer (for example, a wholesaler or a manufacturer) orders from the supplier's website or from an online B2B marketplace; the supplier delivers, and the buyer sells the product through its own outlets or uses it in its own products.

**Examples:**

1. A car manufacturer buying components (tyres, glass) from its suppliers through an online system.
2. A wholesaler ordering products from a company's website and selling them in its retail shops.
3. Online B2B marketplaces such as **Alibaba.com** and **IndiaMART**.
4. Companies buying software and payroll services from other companies.

**Technologies used in B2B:**

1. **EDI (Electronic Data Interchange)** — machine-readable exchange of business documents such as purchase orders and invoices.
2. **Internet**, **intranet** (a private network inside a company) and **extranet** (a network that gives partners, suppliers or customers limited access to the company's network).
3. **Back-end information system integration** — connecting the database systems of the businesses.

**Types of B2B marketplaces:**

1. **Supplier-oriented marketplace** — a supplier sets up the marketplace and sells to many buyers.
2. **Buyer-oriented marketplace** — a buyer runs its own e-market and invites suppliers to bid.
3. **Intermediary-oriented marketplace** — a third party runs the marketplace where many buyers and sellers trade.

**Characteristics:** few customers, but large orders of high value; long-term relationships; several people take part in each buying decision; long sales cycle. B2B accounts for the largest share of e-commerce by value.

### B2C (Business-to-Consumer)

B2C is the e-commerce model in which a **business sells products or services directly to individual consumers (end users)** through its website or app.

It is the most familiar form of e-commerce (online retail).

**How it works (steps of the consumer):**

1. The consumer identifies a need.
2. Searches the website for products that meet the need.
3. Compares similar products on price, delivery date and other terms.
4. Places the order.
5. Pays (online or cash on delivery).
6. Receives and checks the product.
7. Contacts the seller for after-sales service, or returns the product if it is not satisfactory.

**Examples:** **Amazon**, **Daraz**, **Chaldal**, Netflix, online booking of air tickets and hotels.

**Features:** heavy advertising is needed to attract customers; large investment in hardware and software; strong customer support is essential.

B2C supports **disintermediation** — removing intermediaries (wholesalers, distributors, retailers) so that the manufacturer sells directly to the consumer — and **re-intermediation** — new electronic intermediaries such as e-malls and price-comparison sites.

#### Difference Between B2B and B2C

| Basis | B2B | B2C |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buyer | Another business | An individual consumer (end user) |
| Number of customers | Few | Very many |
| Order size and value | Large quantities, high value | Small quantities, low value |
| Decision-making | Several people, including senior managers; rational, based on value and return on investment | One person; quick, often emotional |
| Sales cycle | Long | Short |
| Relationship | Long-term | Short, often one-time |
| Marketing | Informative, through the value chain | Directly to the consumer; advertising and branding |
| Pricing | Negotiated, bulk prices | Fixed retail prices |
| Example | Alibaba.com, a manufacturer buying parts | Amazon, Daraz |

### C2C (Consumer-to-Consumer)

C2C is the e-commerce model in which **consumers sell products and services to other consumers**, usually through a third-party website.

The platform may charge a fee or a commission.

**How it works:** a seller posts an advertisement (for example, a used car, a motorcycle, furniture or a room for rent) on a classified or auction site; buyers search the listings, contact the seller and buy directly. Some platforms hold the payment until the buyer accepts the goods, and use buyer and seller ratings to build trust.

**Examples:** **eBay** (online auctions), **OLX**, **Bikroy.com**, Craigslist and Facebook Marketplace.

### C2B (Consumer-to-Business)

C2B is the e-commerce model in which **individual consumers provide products, services or value to businesses**.

Consumers may also **set the price they are willing to pay**, and businesses compete to serve them. It is the reverse of B2C.

**How it works:** in one form, a consumer states a budget for a service (for example, a loan or a car), and businesses that can meet it within that budget contact the consumer with offers. In another form, individuals sell their work to companies.

**Examples:**

1. Comparing personal or car loan interest rates offered by several banks for the consumer's requirement.
2. Freelancers selling services such as programming, graphic design, writing and photography to companies (through sites such as **Upwork** and **Fiverr**).
3. Bloggers and YouTubers reviewing or promoting a company's products for payment.
4. Customers giving reviews and product ideas to companies.

## B2G and G2C {#b2g-and-g2c}

Models in which the government takes part are part of **e-governance** — the use of information and communication technology (ICT) to provide government services and information.

### B2G (Business-to-Government)

B2G is the e-commerce model in which **businesses sell products and services to government** bodies over the Internet.

It is a **variant of B2B** in which the buyer is a government organization.

**How it works:** the government publishes its requirements (tenders) on a government-approved website; businesses register and submit their bids, applications and documents online; the government evaluates the bids, awards the contract and pays online.

**Examples:**

1. **Electronic government procurement (e-GP)** — government buying goods, works and services through online tenders (Bangladesh has a national e-GP system for public procurement).
2. An engineering company contracting with a city corporation to build a water supply system.
3. A software company supplying software and IT services to a government ministry.
4. Businesses submitting tax returns, licence applications and other forms to government websites.

### G2C (Government-to-Citizen)

G2C is the e-governance model in which the **government provides services and information directly to citizens** through websites and apps.

Its goal is to give citizens access to services **at any time and from anywhere**, and to **reduce the time and cost** of dealing with government offices.

**Services provided:**

1. Registration of **birth, death and marriage** certificates.
2. **Passport** and national ID applications.
3. Online **tax** filing and payment, and payment of utility bills and fees.
4. **Auctions** of government vehicles, machinery and other materials.
5. Public information, examination results, job applications, land records, health and education services.

**Examples:** online birth registration, e-passport applications, online income tax returns and the national portal of government services in Bangladesh.

### Related Government Models

1. **G2B (Government-to-Business)** — the government provides services and information to businesses: licences and registrations, forms, online tenders and auctions. It is the reverse of B2G.
2. **G2G (Government-to-Government)** — exchange of data and services among government departments and agencies, for example through shared online databases.
3. **G2E (Government-to-Employee)** — services for government employees, such as applying for leave and checking salary and leave records.
4. **C2G (Consumer-to-Government)** — citizens paying taxes, fees and utility bills to the government online; the reverse of G2C.

#### Difference Between B2G and G2C

| Basis | B2G | G2C |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seller / provider | Business | Government |
| Buyer / receiver | Government | Citizen (consumer) |
| Nature | Commercial: the government buys from businesses | Service: the government serves citizens |
| Main purpose | Procurement of goods, works and services | Easy, fast, low-cost delivery of public services |
| Typical activity | Online tenders, bids, contracts | Certificates, passports, tax, bill payment, information |
| Example | e-GP online tendering | Online birth registration |
