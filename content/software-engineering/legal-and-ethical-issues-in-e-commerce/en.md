### Legal and Ethical Issues

E-commerce raises two kinds of issues:

1. **Legal issues** — questions that are governed by **law**: whether an online contract is valid, which country's court has authority, who owns the content, how personal data must be protected and what happens when a seller cheats a buyer. Breaking a law brings **legal punishment** (a fine, imprisonment or compensation).
2. **Ethical issues** — questions of **right and wrong behaviour** that are not always covered by law: honest advertising, fair prices, respect for customers' privacy and equal access for everyone. Breaking an ethical rule brings **loss of trust and reputation**, even if it is not illegal.

Something can be legal but unethical (for example, collecting more customer data than needed when no law forbids it), and laws often lag behind new technology.

### Cyber Law

Cyber law (Internet law) is the **part of the legal system that deals with the Internet, computers, e-commerce and cyberspace**.

It covers electronic contracts and signatures, intellectual property online, privacy and data protection, and cybercrime. Cyber laws make online agreements valid and enforceable, protect the parties to e-commerce and provide for punishment of cybercrimes. Laws differ from country to country, which makes enforcement on the global Internet difficult.

Examples of cyber laws are the **Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA), 1986** of the USA, the **Information Technology Act, 2000** of India, the **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)** of the European Union and the **Information and Communication Technology Act, 2006** of Bangladesh.

### Legal Issues in E-Commerce

The main legal issues in e-commerce are:

1. **Electronic contracts** — whether an agreement made online (for example, by clicking an "I Accept" button or placing an order) is valid and enforceable; the terms and conditions, order acceptance, pricing information and cancellation rules must be clear.
2. **Electronic records and digital signatures** — the law must recognize electronic documents and digital signatures as equal to paper documents and handwritten signatures. Digital signatures are issued through licensed **certifying authorities**.
3. **Jurisdiction** — a website can be reached from every country, but laws are national; when a buyer and a seller are in different countries, it is difficult to decide **which country's law applies and which court can hear a dispute**.
4. **Intellectual property rights (IPR)** — protection of creations of the mind on the Internet:
   - **Copyright** — protects original works such as text, images, music, videos and software; copying a website's content or selling pirated software infringes it.
   - **Trademark** — protects brand names and logos; using another company's brand to sell goods is infringement.
   - **Patent** — protects inventions, including some technical processes.
   - **Trade secrets** — confidential business information.
   - **Domain names and cybersquatting** — registering a domain name that is the same as, or similar to, another party's trademark in bad faith, to sell it at a high price or to mislead customers.

   A website must protect its own intellectual property and must not violate the rights of others, especially when users upload content.
5. **Privacy and data protection** — the collection, storage, use and sharing of customers' personal data (names, addresses, card details, browsing history) must be lawful, and customers must be told how their data is used (a **privacy policy**). Laws such as the EU's GDPR impose heavy fines for misuse.
6. **Consumer protection** — buyers must be protected from **fake products, misleading advertisements, wrong descriptions, non-delivery, late delivery and refusal of refunds**; sellers must give correct information about the product, price and seller, and must handle complaints.
7. **Online payment and fraud** — security of payments, liability for unauthorized transactions, credit card fraud and refunds.
8. **Taxation** — collecting VAT and other taxes on online sales, especially cross-border sales of digital goods, because online sales are hard for tax authorities to track (risk of **tax evasion**).
9. **Cybercrime** — hacking, phishing, identity theft, online fraud, spreading malware and denial of service, which must be defined as crimes and punished.
10. **Spam** — unsolicited bulk commercial e-mail and SMS, which many countries restrict.
11. **Liability, warranties and disclaimers** — who is responsible when a product is faulty or when online information is wrong; sellers use written warranties and disclaimers to define their liability.
12. **Regulated goods** — the sale of medicines, weapons and other restricted goods online, and export rules for goods sent abroad.

### Ethical Issues in E-Commerce

The ethical issues of the information age are often grouped under **four headings: privacy, property, accuracy and accessibility** (with **security** as a fifth):

1. **Privacy** — the right of a person to control information about himself or herself; it includes **secrecy**, **anonymity** and **solitude**. Ethical problems are collecting customer data without consent, tracking users with **cookies** and spyware, building profiles from browsing history, and selling customer data to others.
2. **Property** — respecting intellectual property: not copying content, images or software (**piracy**), not selling counterfeit branded goods, and giving fair credit and payment to creators.
3. **Accuracy** — giving correct and complete information about products, prices and the seller, and taking responsibility when online information is wrong.
4. **Accessibility** — fair access to information and services for everyone; the **digital divide** (the gap between people who have Internet access and digital skills and those who do not, such as poor, rural, elderly and illiterate people) and access for people with disabilities.
5. **Security** — protecting customers' data and money from theft and misuse, while balancing security with individual rights.

Other ethical issues in e-commerce are:

1. **Honest advertising** — no false claims, hidden charges, **fake reviews** or misleading discounts.
2. **Fair pricing** — no price discrimination that unfairly charges different customers different prices (dynamic pricing based on a user's browsing history raises fairness questions).
3. **Spam and intrusive marketing** — sending unwanted messages, and manipulative designs that trick users into buying or subscribing.
4. **Harmful actions** — deliberately damaging or altering other people's files, programs or websites.
5. **Fair treatment of sellers, delivery workers and employees** on e-commerce platforms.

### Difference Between Legal and Ethical Issues

| Basis | Legal issues | Ethical issues |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Based on | Laws made by the government | Moral principles of right and wrong |
| Nature | Compulsory; must be obeyed | Voluntary; depends on conscience and social values |
| Breaking it brings | Fines, imprisonment or compensation | Loss of trust, reputation and customers |
| Written | In laws and regulations | Usually in codes of conduct, or unwritten |
| Scope | Same for everyone in a country | Can differ between people and cultures |
| Example | Selling pirated software; stealing card data | Collecting unnecessary customer data; misleading but legal advertising |

### Laws in Bangladesh Related to E-Commerce

1. **Information and Communication Technology Act, 2006** — gives legal recognition to **electronic records and digital signatures**, provides for the **Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA)**, which licenses the certifying authorities that issue digital signature certificates, and defines cybercrimes, which are tried by cyber tribunals. It was amended in 2013.
2. **Digital Security Act, 2018** → **Cyber Security Act, 2023** → **Cyber Security Ordinance, 2025** — laws on digital security and cybercrime. The Digital Security Act, 2018 was replaced by the Cyber Security Act, 2023, which was repealed in May 2025 by the interim government and replaced by the Cyber Security Ordinance, 2025.
3. **Consumer Rights Protection Act, 2009** — protects consumers against adulterated and fake products, overcharging, false advertising and fraud; it set up the **Directorate of National Consumer Rights Protection (DNCRP)**, to which consumers (including online shoppers) can complain.
4. **Digital Commerce Operation Guidelines, 2021** (Ministry of Commerce) — rules for e-commerce businesses: correct product information, delivery within **5 days in the same city** and **10 days in a different city**, refunds through the same payment method, and limits on advance payment — full advance payment only through a **Bangladesh Bank-approved escrow service**, which holds the customer's money and releases it to the seller after delivery is confirmed.
5. **Copyright and trademark laws** protect intellectual property in Bangladesh, including content and brands used online.
