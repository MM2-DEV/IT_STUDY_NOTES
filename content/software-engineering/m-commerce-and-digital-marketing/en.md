## Mobile Commerce {#mobile-commerce}

### Definition of M-Commerce

M-commerce (mobile commerce) is the **buying and selling of goods and services through wireless handheld devices** such as smartphones and tablets.

It also includes making payments over a wireless network. It is a **subset of e-commerce**: the same activities carried out on mobile devices, so that users can shop and pay **anywhere, at any time**, without a fixed Internet connection.

**Examples:** shopping in the Amazon or Daraz mobile app, buying an app from the Google Play Store or Apple App Store, mobile banking and MFS payments (bKash, Nagad), booking tickets with a mobile app, and ordering food through a delivery app.

### Applications of M-Commerce

1. **Mobile shopping** — buying products through mobile apps and mobile websites.
2. **Mobile banking and mobile financial services** — checking balances, transferring money and paying bills.
3. **Mobile payments** — mobile wallets, QR-code payments and **NFC** contactless payments.
4. **Mobile ticketing and booking** — air, train, bus and movie tickets, and hotel reservations.
5. **Purchase of digital content** — apps, games, music, e-books, ringtones and subscriptions.
6. **Location-based services** — offers and services based on the user's GPS location (nearby shops, ride-sharing, food delivery).
7. **Mobile auctions, mobile marketing and mobile entertainment.**

### How a Business Sells Through M-Commerce

1. **Decide where to sell** — through third-party outlets or aggregators (which charge a commission), through **app stores** (Google Play, App Store), or through its **own mobile store** (a mobile website or app).
2. **Set up mobile billing** — through a third-party payment service such as PayPal (which charges a commission) or its own payment gateway.
3. **Make the site mobile-friendly** — easy to use with touch screens on small displays.

### Mobile Payment Methods

1. **Mobile wallets** and mobile financial services (bKash, Nagad, Apple Pay, Google Pay).
2. **NFC (Near Field Communication)** — tapping the phone on a payment terminal.
3. **QR-code payments.**
4. **Card payments** inside apps.
5. **Carrier (mobile operator) billing** — the charge is added to the phone bill or deducted from the airtime balance.

### Advantages of M-Commerce

1. **Portability and ubiquity** — shopping and payment from anywhere, at any time.
2. **Instant access** — faster transactions; payments take a few seconds.
3. **Location-based services** — offers suited to where the user is.
4. **Personalization** — content and offers based on the user's preferences.
5. **Push notifications** — the business can reach the customer instantly with alerts and offers.
6. **Better user experience** — apps can use the camera, GPS and **biometric authentication** (fingerprint, face).
7. **Integration with social media.**
8. **Wider reach** — far more people own mobile phones than computers.

### Disadvantages of M-Commerce

1. **Device limitations** — small screens and keypads, limited processing power and battery life.
2. **Security risks** — lost or stolen phones, phishing messages and mobile malware.
3. **Connectivity dependence** — needs a good mobile network; the connection can be slow or costly.
4. **Higher development cost** — apps must be built and maintained for different platforms and devices.
5. **Privacy concerns** — apps may track location and personal data.

### Difference Between E-Commerce and M-Commerce

| Basis | E-commerce | M-commerce |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning | Buying and selling over the Internet using electronic devices | Buying and selling using wireless handheld (mobile) devices |
| Scope | Broader concept | A subset of e-commerce |
| Devices | Mainly desktop and laptop computers | Smartphones, tablets and other handheld devices |
| Mobility | Usually from a fixed place | From anywhere (location-independent) |
| Portability | Devices are not easy to carry | Devices are easy to carry |
| Connection | Usually a wired or fixed Internet connection | Wireless: mobile data (3G/4G/5G) or Wi-Fi |
| Interface | Large screens, full websites | Small touch screens, mobile apps and mobile websites |
| Location tracking | Limited | Good (GPS, mobile network) |
| Push notifications | Limited | Easily sent through apps |
| Payment methods | Cards, Internet banking, digital wallets | Also mobile wallets, MFS, NFC, QR codes and carrier billing |
| Reach | Comparatively lower | Higher, because more people have mobile phones |
| Development | Earlier (e-commerce dates to the 1970s) | Later (from the 1990s) |
| Examples | Shopping on the Amazon website from a computer | Shopping in the Daraz app; paying with bKash |

## Online Advertising and Digital Marketing {#online-advertising-and-digital-marketing}

### Definition of Digital Marketing

Digital marketing is the **promotion of products, services or brands through digital channels** such as search engines, websites, social media, e-mail and mobile apps.

Its aim is to reach target customers and turn them into buyers.

### Definition of Online Advertising

Online advertising is a form of **promotion that uses the Internet to deliver advertisements to customers**.

The advertisements appear in search engines, websites, social media, e-mail and mobile apps. It is a part of digital marketing: the **paid** part, in which advertisers pay publishers (websites, search engines and apps) for ad space.

The parties in online advertising are:

1. **Advertiser** — the business that pays to show the advertisement.
2. **Publisher** — the website, app or platform that shows the advertisement in its ad space.
3. **Ad network / ad exchange** — the system that connects advertisers with publishers (for example, Google Ads).
4. **Audience (user)** — the people who see and click the advertisement.

### Types of Digital Marketing

The main types (channels) of digital marketing are:

1. **Search Engine Optimization (SEO)** — improving a website's content and structure so that it ranks higher in the **organic (unpaid)** results of search engines such as Google. It has two parts:
   - **On-page SEO** — work on the website itself: titles and meta descriptions, quality content and keywords, headings, internal links, image optimization, clean URLs, a sitemap, fast loading and mobile-friendliness.
   - **Off-page SEO** — work outside the website, mainly earning **backlinks** (links from other sites), together with social media sharing, guest posts and influencer outreach.
2. **Search Engine Marketing (SEM)** — buying **paid advertisements** that appear in search engine results for chosen keywords (for example, Google Ads, Microsoft Bing Ads).
3. **Pay-Per-Click (PPC) advertising** — an advertising model in which the advertiser **pays only when a user clicks** the advertisement; used in search, display, social and mobile advertising.
4. **Social Media Marketing (SMM)** — building brand awareness, engagement and sales through social platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, LinkedIn and X (Twitter), using posts and paid social ads.
5. **E-mail marketing** — sending promotional and informational e-mails (offers, newsletters, order updates) to a list of subscribers who have agreed to receive them.
6. **Content marketing** — creating and sharing useful content (blogs, articles, videos, infographics, podcasts, e-books) to attract and keep customers.
7. **Affiliate marketing** — partners (affiliates) promote the seller's products through special referral links and earn a **commission** on every sale they bring.
8. **Influencer marketing** — popular people on social media (influencers) promote products to their followers.
9. **Video marketing** — using videos (for example, on YouTube) to promote, explain and engage.
10. **Mobile marketing** — promoting through mobile devices: SMS and MMS messages, mobile apps, mobile websites, in-app ads, QR codes and location-based offers.
11. **Remarketing (retargeting)** — showing ads again to people who have already visited the website or viewed a product.
12. **Marketing automation and web analytics** — software that automates campaigns, and tools such as Google Analytics that measure visitors and results.

### Types of Online Advertisements

1. **Search (text) ads** — short text ads shown with search results for a keyword.
2. **Display / banner ads** — graphic (image or animated) advertisements placed on web pages, in standard sizes such as the 728 × 90 **leaderboard**, the 468 × 60 **full banner**, the 160 × 600 **skyscraper** and the 300 × 250 / 336 × 280 **rectangle**.
3. **Pop-up ads** — open in a new small window over the web page (a pop-under opens behind it).
4. **Rich media ads** — interactive ads with animation, audio, video or games that the user can expand or play.
5. **Video ads** — played before, during or after online videos (for example, on YouTube).
6. **Social media ads** — paid posts and ads on Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn and others.
7. **E-mail ads** — ads in newsletters and promotional e-mails.
8. **Mobile ads** — mobile web banners, text ads, **SMS/MMS ads**, rich media mobile ads, mobile video ads and **in-app ads**.

### Pricing Models of Online Advertising

1. **CPC (Cost Per Click)**, also called **PPC (Pay Per Click)** — the advertiser pays each time a user clicks the ad.
2. **CPM (Cost Per Mille)** — the advertiser pays for every **thousand impressions** (displays) of the ad, whether or not it is clicked ("mille" is Latin for thousand).
3. **CPA (Cost Per Action / Acquisition)** — the advertiser pays only when a user completes an action, such as a purchase or a sign-up.

#### Formulas of Advertising Metrics

> **CTR = (Number of clicks ÷ Number of impressions) × 100%**

> **Cost of a CPM campaign = (Number of impressions ÷ 1000) × CPM rate**

> **Effective CPC = Total cost ÷ Number of clicks**

> **CPA = Total cost ÷ Number of conversions (actions)**

> **Conversion rate = (Number of conversions ÷ Number of clicks) × 100%**

> **ROI = ((Revenue − Cost) ÷ Cost) × 100%**

Here:

1. **Impression** — one display of the ad on a user's screen.
2. **Click** — one click on the ad by a user.
3. **CTR (Click-Through Rate)** — the percentage of impressions that lead to a click; a higher CTR means the ad is more relevant to the audience.
4. **Conversion** — a desired action after the click, such as a purchase.
5. **ROI (Return on Investment)** — the profit earned on the advertising cost, as a percentage.

#### Worked Example

An online shop runs a banner ad at a **CPM rate of Tk 200**. The ad gets **50,000 impressions** and **750 clicks**, which lead to **25 sales** with a total revenue of **Tk 30,000**.

1. **Cost:** (50,000 ÷ 1000) × 200 = 50 × 200 = **Tk 10,000**
2. **CTR:** (750 ÷ 50,000) × 100% = **1.5%**
3. **Effective CPC:** 10,000 ÷ 750 = **Tk 13.33** per click
4. **CPA:** 10,000 ÷ 25 = **Tk 400** per sale
5. **Conversion rate:** (25 ÷ 750) × 100% = **3.33%**
6. **ROI:** ((30,000 − 10,000) ÷ 10,000) × 100% = **200%**

### Advantages of Digital Marketing

1. **Wide (global) reach** at a low cost.
2. **24 × 7 availability** — the business is visible at all times.
3. **Targeting** — ads can be shown to people of a chosen age, location, interest or behaviour.
4. **Measurable results** — clicks, visits, sign-ups and sales can be measured immediately with analytics tools.
5. **Cost-effective** — cheaper than most traditional advertising; small businesses can start with a small budget.
6. **Two-way communication** — customers can respond, comment and give feedback; long-term relationships are built.
7. **Personalization and flexibility** — campaigns can be changed quickly, and several campaigns can run at once.
8. **Multimedia** — text, images, audio and video make ads more attractive and persuasive.

### Disadvantages of Digital Marketing

1. **Needs skilled people** — expertise in SEO, advertising, content and analytics.
2. **Negative feedback is public** — complaints on social media can harm the brand quickly.
3. **Online fraud** — fake websites, fake logos and click fraud.
4. **Ad clutter and ad blockers** — too many ads annoy users, and many users block ads.
5. **Dependence on the Internet** — people without Internet access (for example, in remote areas) cannot be reached.
6. **Privacy concerns** about tracking user data.
7. **High competition** — many businesses compete for the same keywords and audience.

### Traditional Marketing vs Digital Marketing

| Basis | Traditional marketing | Digital marketing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Channels | Newspapers, magazines, TV, radio, billboards, leaflets, direct mail | Search engines, websites, social media, e-mail, mobile apps, online ads |
| Reach | Local or limited audience | Local to global; millions at once |
| Availability | Only at fixed times (broadcast or print date) | 24 × 7 |
| Communication | One-way | Two-way (interaction and feedback) |
| Targeting | Broad; hard to target | Precise targeting by age, location and interest |
| Measurement | Difficult to measure | Easy to measure with analytics tools |
| Cost | Usually expensive | Usually cheaper; flexible budget |
| Changes | Hard to change once published | Can be changed at any time |
| Customer feedback | Little and slow | Much and instant |
| Internet needed | No | Yes |
