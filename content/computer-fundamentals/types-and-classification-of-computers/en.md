## Types: Analog Computer, Digital Computer, Hybrid Computer {#types-analog-computer-digital-computer-hybrid-computer}

According to the way they represent and process data, computers are of three types:

1. **Analog computer**
2. **Digital computer**
3. **Hybrid computer**

### Analog Computer

**Definition:** An analog computer is a computer that processes **continuous (analog) data**. It represents numbers by continuously varying **physical quantities**, such as electrical voltage or current, mechanical movement (the rotation of gears or shafts), pressure or temperature, and solves a problem by building a physical model of it.

**Characteristics:**

1. Works with **continuous data**, not discrete values.
2. Represents data by physical quantities such as voltage, current, rotation, pressure or temperature.
3. Gives results as continuous readings, for example the position of a needle on a scale, a graph, or a voltage.
4. Works in **real time**, and is especially suited to solving **differential equations** and simulating physical systems.
5. Has **limited accuracy**: results are usually accurate to only three or four significant figures, and they are affected by noise and by imperfections in the components.
6. Has very limited **memory** for storing data.
7. Is usually built for a **special purpose**. A problem is set up by connecting components, not by writing a program.

**Examples:**

1. Speedometer (everyday analog measuring device)
2. Thermometer (everyday analog measuring device)
3. Slide rule
4. Differential analyser (mechanical analog computer, 1928–1931)
5. Tide-predicting machine (built by Lord Kelvin in 1872)
6. Norden bombsight (used in aircraft)

**Uses:**

1. Simulation of physical systems
2. Solving differential equations
3. Scientific research and engineering
4. Control systems in aircraft, ships and submarines

### Digital Computer

**Definition:** A digital computer is a computer that processes **discrete data**. It represents all data and instructions using the **binary number system**, which has only two digits: **0 and 1** (in the circuit, *off* and *on*).

**Characteristics:**

1. Works with **discrete (digital) values**, not continuous quantities.
2. Represents all data (numbers, text, pictures, sound) in **binary form** (0 and 1).
3. Performs both **arithmetic** and **logical** operations.
4. Gives **highly accurate** results.
5. Has a **large memory** and can store huge amounts of data.
6. Is **programmable** and **general-purpose**: it can do many different jobs by running different programs.
7. Shows results as numbers, text or pictures on a display screen or printer.
8. It is the most common type of computer in use today.

**Examples:**

1. Personal computers (desktops and laptops)
2. Tablets
3. Smartphones
4. Servers
5. Mainframes
6. Supercomputers

**Uses:** Almost everything, for example:

1. Simple calculations
2. Word processing
3. The Internet
4. Business data processing
5. Complex scientific simulation

### Hybrid Computer

**Definition:** A hybrid computer is a computer that combines the features of both **analog and digital computers**. It can process both continuous (analog) data and discrete (digital) data.

**How it works:**

- The **analog part** handles continuous, real-world quantities such as voltage, current, temperature or pressure, and quickly solves complex mathematical problems such as differential equations.
- The **digital part** acts as the **controller**: it performs the logical and numerical operations, stores data and gives precise results.
- When analog signals are taken as input, they are converted into digital form for processing.

**Characteristics:**

1. Combines the **real-time speed** of analog computers with the **precision** and **versatility** of digital computers.
2. Can process both continuous and discrete data.
3. Can solve complex equations very quickly and analyse data in **real time**.
4. Usually used for **special purposes**.
5. More **complex** and **more expensive** than analog or digital computers alone.

**Examples:**

1. **Hycomp 250** (Packard Bell, 1961): the first desktop hybrid computer.
2. **HYDAC 2400** (EAI, 1963): an early integrated hybrid computer.
3. **ECG (electrocardiogram) machine** (hospital)
4. **Ultrasound machine** (hospital)
5. **Patient monitoring system** (hospital)

The hospital machines measure analog signals from the body (such as heartbeat, blood pressure and temperature) and process and store them digitally.

**Uses:**

1. Hospitals (patient monitoring, ECG, ultrasound)
2. Petrol pumps (measuring fuel flow and showing the quantity and price digitally)
3. Airplanes
4. Scientific research and simulation
5. Weather prediction
6. Industrial process control and control systems

### Differences Between Analog and Digital Computers

| Basis | Analog Computer | Digital Computer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data | Works with continuous data | Works with discrete data |
| Representation | Physical quantities (voltage, rotation, pressure) | Binary digits (0 and 1) |
| Accuracy | Lower (about three or four significant figures) | Very high |
| Memory | Very limited | Large |
| Programming | Set up by connecting components; not programmable in the usual sense | Programmable with software |
| Purpose | Usually special-purpose | General-purpose |
| Reliability | Less reliable | More reliable |
| Output | Continuous readings (meter, graph, voltage) | Numbers, text and pictures on a screen |
| Examples | Speedometer, thermometer, slide rule, differential analyser | Personal computer, laptop, smartphone, supercomputer |

## Classification: Microcomputer, Mainframe Computer, Supercomputer {#classification-microcomputer-mainframe-computer-supercomputer}

According to their **size, capacity, speed and cost**, digital computers are classified as follows (from the largest and most powerful to the smallest):

1. **Supercomputer**
2. **Mainframe computer**
3. **Minicomputer**
4. **Microcomputer**

Minicomputers are mid-sized computers between mainframes and microcomputers. The three main classes are explained below.

### Microcomputer

**Definition:** A microcomputer is a small, low-cost computer whose central processing unit (CPU) is a **single microprocessor chip**. It is designed to be used by **one person at a time**, so it is also called a **personal computer (PC)**.

**Characteristics:**

1. The **smallest** and **least expensive** class of computer.
2. Its CPU is a single **microprocessor**.
3. Designed for a **single user**.
4. Easy to use, and can be used at home, in school or in the office.
5. Many microcomputers are **portable** (laptops, tablets, smartphones).
6. Less powerful than minicomputers and mainframes.

**History:** Microcomputers became popular in the 1970s and 1980s as microprocessors became more powerful. The French **Micral N** (1973) is regarded as the first microprocessor-based microcomputer available for sale. The Apple II was a well-known early example.

**Examples:**

1. Desktop computers
2. Laptops (notebooks)
3. Tablet computers
4. Smartphones

**Uses:**

1. Personal and office work (word processing, spreadsheets, Internet browsing and e-mail)
2. Education
3. Entertainment
4. Small business work

### Mainframe Computer

**Definition:** A mainframe computer is a **very large, powerful and expensive** computer used mainly by **large organizations** for critical applications, such as processing huge amounts of data and very large numbers of transactions.

**Characteristics:**

1. Very large in size and very expensive.
2. Can serve **hundreds or even thousands of users at the same time**.
3. Can run many programs at the same time.
4. Very **reliable** and highly **available**: its mean time between failures is measured in decades.
5. High **security** for data.
6. Can store and process **very large databases**.
7. Its processing speed is sometimes measured in **MIPS** (Million Instructions Per Second).
8. Less powerful in computation than a supercomputer.

**Examples:**

1. IBM Z series (IBM is the main manufacturer of mainframes)
2. Unisys ClearPath
3. Fujitsu GS21

**Uses:**

1. Banks
2. Insurance companies
3. Stock exchanges
4. Railway and airline ticket reservation
5. Census data processing
6. Other government and large business organizations that need large-scale transaction processing

### Supercomputer

**Definition:** A supercomputer is the **most powerful, fastest and most expensive** type of computer. It is used for extremely complex calculations that ordinary computers cannot perform in a reasonable time.

**Characteristics:**

1. The **fastest** and **most powerful** class of computer.
2. Uses a very large number of **processors working in parallel** (parallel processing).
3. Its speed is measured in **FLOPS** (Floating-point Operations Per Second), for example teraFLOPS (10¹² FLOPS), petaFLOPS (10¹⁵ FLOPS) and exaFLOPS (10¹⁸ FLOPS).
4. **Very expensive**, and needs a special environment with controlled cooling.
5. Usually built for a few specific, calculation-heavy tasks.
6. Since November 2017, all of the world's 500 fastest supercomputers have run **Linux-based** operating systems.

**History and facts:**

- The **CDC 6600** (Control Data Corporation, **1964**), designed by **Seymour Cray**, is generally considered the **first successful supercomputer**.
- Since 2022, the fastest supercomputers have been **exascale**: they can perform more than **10¹⁸ (one quintillion) floating-point operations per second**.
- The **TOP500** project ranks the world's 500 fastest supercomputers **twice a year** (June and November).

**Examples:**

1. CDC 6600
2. Cray XT5
3. IBM Blue Gene
4. Sierra
5. Fugaku

**Uses:**

1. Weather forecasting and climate research
2. Scientific research and simulation
3. Nuclear energy and weapons research
4. Space research
5. Molecular modelling and genetic engineering
6. Analysis of huge amounts of data

### Comparison of Microcomputer, Mainframe and Supercomputer

| Basis | Microcomputer | Mainframe Computer | Supercomputer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Size | Smallest | Very large | Largest |
| Speed and power | Lowest of the three | Very high | Highest |
| Cost | Cheapest | Very expensive | Most expensive |
| Users | One user at a time | Hundreds or thousands at a time | Used for a few large tasks |
| Speed measured in | — | MIPS | FLOPS |
| Main use | Personal and office work | Large-scale data and transaction processing | Complex scientific calculations |
| Examples | Desktop, laptop, smartphone | IBM Z series | CDC 6600, Fugaku |
